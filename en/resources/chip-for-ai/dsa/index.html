DSA (Domain-specific Architecture)

Specialized accelerators, model-specific chips, and compute-in-memory architectures for AI workloads.

Domain-specific Accelerators

  • Etched — Co-designs chips, racks, interconnects, and software for large-model inference, with material on low-voltage inference and cluster-level memory.
  • Gemmini (2019/2021) — A parameterized systolic-array accelerator generator that combines RTL, a RISC-V host, memory systems, and software for full-system DNN evaluation. Paper
  • Jalapeño (2026) — OpenAI’s example of co-designing chips, memory, networking, and inference software, also describing the use of AI in hardware design and kernel optimization.
  • Taalas HC1 (2026) — Hardwires specific models into silicon; the HC1 demonstration runs Llama 3.1 8B, with official material on its architecture and inference approach.

CIM (Compute-in-Memory)

  • AccelForge / HWComponents — Carry forward CiMLoop architecture and component models to evaluate mappings, area, energy, and latency for accelerators including compute-in-memory; the original CiMLoop repository is no longer maintained. Component Models Original CiMLoop Repository CiMLoop Paper
  • CrossSim — Evaluates how analog crossbar nonidealities, including device errors, drift, noise, and ADC effects, affect algorithm accuracy, with hardware-aware training support.
  • DNN+NeuroSim — Combines device, circuit, and DNN models to assess the accuracy, performance, energy, and area of compute-in-memory accelerators.
  • IBM AIHWKit / AIHWKit-Lightning — Models analog compute-in-memory noise and nonidealities in PyTorch, supporting hardware-aware training. AIHWKit-Lightning
