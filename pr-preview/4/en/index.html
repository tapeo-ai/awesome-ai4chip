We will continue building Awesome AI4Chip into the most comprehensive source of AI for Chip news and resources, collecting papers, open-source projects, datasets, benchmarks, tools, products, and technical articles. The goal is to help readers from different backgrounds quickly understand the field: identify key problems and technical approaches, find learning material and reproducible projects, and follow research and industry developments.

Guide

AI Model

For readers familiar with LLMs who want to research RTL generation, domain post-training, or evaluation.

  1. LLMs for EDA: A Survey — Covers EDA tasks, models, data, and evaluation, offering a quick overview of key directions at the intersection of LLMs and EDA.
  2. VerilogEval — Follow the README’s spec-to-rtl workflow to understand evaluation from specifications through Verilog to simulation results.
  3. CodeV-R1 — Use this Verilog post-training project to explore the training configurations in sft/, then the RL workflow and testbench reward in verl/. Compare these with the released data to understand how reasoning distillation and simulation feedback enter training.

If reading simple RTL is still difficult, begin with combinational and sequential logic exercises in HDLBits, then cocotb First Steps to connect Python testbenches with clocks, reset, assertions, and waveforms. Continue through Data, Benchmark, or Training for your research question.

AI EDA

For readers with RTL / EDA experience who want to build design agents or automated optimization flows.

  1. The Dawn of Agentic EDA — Start with section 2 on agent architectures, then choose section 3 on RTL / Verification or section 4 on Physical Design.
  2. OpenROAD Flow Scripts Tutorial — Use the Ibex / SKY130 example to understand the complete RTL-to-GDS flow.
  3. OpenROAD — Running Claude Code in Docker — Connect a coding agent to a container with EDA tools and see how it accesses a project, builds, and runs tests.

For synthesis optimization, continue with Yosys Synthesis Starter to follow a FIFO from RTL to a netlist. For verification, use SymbiYosys Getting Started to learn assertions, bounded checks, proofs, and counterexamples. Find projects in AI EDA and EDA Optimization.

Analog / Mixed-Signal

Readers with circuit fundamentals who want an analog design and optimization environment can follow JKU Analog Circuit Design: chapters 1 and 2 introduce Xschem, ngspice, and an open PDK; chapter 3 covers gm/ID sizing; chapter 8 develops OTA simulation, PVT, and Monte Carlo analysis. The course includes circuit files and Python notebooks.

To connect simulation to parameter search or agents, continue with the looping, parameter modification, and data export sections of the ngspice Control Language Tutorial.

Chip for AI

For readers with machine learning or computer architecture foundations who want to study model–accelerator co-design.

  1. MIT 6.5940 — TinyML and Efficient AI Computing — A foundation for studying hardware-aware ML.
  2. Timeloop / Accelergy v4 and its exercises — Learn DSE without first implementing an RTL accelerator.

To study an accelerator’s hardware and software together, continue with Gemmini Quick Start: start with matrix multiplication in the simulator, then read Architecture for the systolic array, scratchpad, accumulator, and dataflow. Explore Hardware-aware ML, DSE, DSA, or Kernel Optimization for further work.
